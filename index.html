1The front door is wider than you think

What is an ATAR?

The Australian Tertiary Admission Rank is a rank, not a mark out of 100. It runs from 0 to 99.95 and shows where a student stands against everyone their age in their state.

An ATAR of 80 means you did better than 80% of your age group. Universities publish the lowest ATAR they accepted for each course.

Most people go to university for a better jobm so the job market shapes who applies. When work is hard to find, study looks like the better option. When employers are hiring, some people take the job instead.

The last five years show this clearly. COVID pushed unemployment to 6.5% in 2020, and applications for 2021 jumped to 341,983. By 2022 unemployment had fallen to 3.7%, and the following year only 300,111 people applied, the fewest since 2010.

The rise to a peak in 2017 had another cause as well. From 2012 to 2017, universities were funded for as many bachelor's places as they chose to offer, so they kept growing.

2Where you apply matters

Demand did not grow evenly. In 2025, applications rose in six of the eight states and territories, led by the Northern Territory and Tasmania, while Western Australia and South Australia stayed flat.

Within states, applicants concentrate in a few cities. Melbourne's universities alone drew one in four applicants, far more than Sydney's, even though more people apply in New South Wales overall. Much of New South Wales's demand goes to regional universities instead, such as Newcastle and Charles Sturt. Melbourne is also home to Monash, the same university as Monash Malaysia.

The best-known universities are also the hardest to enter. The Group of Eight, Australia's leading research universities, including Monash, Melbourne and Sydney, attracted a quarter of all applicants in 2025 but made only 73 offers for every 100 of them. Every other group made between 87 and 91.

3What people want to study has flipped

Over fifteen years, applicants have moved towards health, science and technology. Between 2010 and 2025, applications for information technology rose 59% and for the sciences 50%, while creative arts lost 39%. Health overtook society and culture in 2014 and has been the most popular field ever since.
